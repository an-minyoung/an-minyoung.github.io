Research approach

I am a sociologist and postdoctoral fellow at Stanford’s Shorenstein Asia-Pacific Research Center. My work asks how social environments shape consequential decisions: where people move, which careers they pursue, and how they understand their place in society.

I combine large-scale population and survey data with computational methods and in-depth interviews. My current research examines skilled migration, the return decisions of U.S.-trained PhDs, and gendered career pathways. I received my PhD in Sociology from the University of Arizona in 2025, where I also earned a Certificate in Computational Social Science.

I use SQL, R, Python, and Stata for quantitative and computational social science research.

At the University of Arizona, I taught The Good Society (Gen Ed); Introduction to Sociology (two semesters); and Gender, Power, Inequality as instructor of record. I also served as a teaching assistant across seven courses, including quantitative reasoning, qualitative health-data analysis, and courses on families, inequality, reproduction, and care.
